## 2. Model Specification

### Exact TDGL Equation (Model A)
The system is governed by the real scalar, nonconserved time-dependent Ginzburg–Landau equation (Model A):
$$\frac{\partial \phi(\mathbf{r}, t)}{\partial t} = \phi(\mathbf{r}, t) - \phi^3(\mathbf{r}, t) + \nabla^2 \phi(\mathbf{r}, t)$$

- **1D Formulation**:
  $$\frac{\partial \phi(x, t)}{\partial t} = \phi(x, t) - \phi(x, t)^3 + \frac{\partial^2 \phi(x, t)}{\partial x^2}$$
- **2D Formulation**:
  $$\frac{\partial \phi(x, y, t)}{\partial t} = \phi(x, y, t) - \phi(x, y, t)^3 + \frac{\partial^2 \phi(x, y, t)}{\partial x^2} + \frac{\partial^2 \phi(x, y, t)}{\partial y^2}$$

### Physical Interpretation of Order Parameter $\phi$
- $\phi(\mathbf{r}, t)$ is a real scalar order parameter representing the coarse-grained local magnetization of an uniaxially anisotropic ferromagnet (Ising-like spin system) quenched below its critical temperature $T_c$.
- It is non-conserved: $\int \phi(\mathbf{r}, t) \, d\mathbf{r}$ changes over time as domains grow and merge.
- This is strictly distinct from the complex order parameter $\psi$ of superconductivity or superfluidity; no magnetic vector potential $\mathbf{A}$ or scalar potential $\Phi$ appears.

## 3. Free-Energy Functional and Derivation

The dynamics is purely relaxational (gradient flow) generated by the Ginzburg–Landau free energy functional:
$$F[\phi] = \int_{\Omega} \left[ -\frac{\phi^2}{2} + \frac{\phi^4}{4} + \frac{1}{2} |\nabla \phi|^2 \right] d\mathbf{r}$$

The local potential $V(\phi) = -\frac{\phi^2}{2} + \frac{\phi^4}{4} = \frac{1}{4}(\phi^2 - 1)^2 - \frac{1}{4}$ represents a symmetric double-well potential with minima at $\phi = \pm 1$.

The relaxational kinetic equation is:
$$\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t} = -\frac{\delta F}{\delta \phi}$$
Evaluating the functional derivative:
$$\frac{\delta F}{\delta \phi} = \frac{\partial f}{\partial \phi} - \nabla \cdot \frac{\partial f}{\partial (\nabla \phi)} = (-\phi + \phi^3) - \nabla^2 \phi$$
Therefore:
$$\frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t} = -(-\phi + \phi^3 - \nabla^2 \phi) = \phi - \phi^3 + \nabla^2 \phi$$

### Monotonic Dissipation of Free Energy
Multiplying by $\partial \phi / \partial t$ and integrating over the domain $\Omega$ with periodic boundary conditions:
$$\frac{dF}{dt} = \int_{\Omega} \frac{\delta F}{\delta \phi} \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t} d\mathbf{r} = -\int_{\Omega} \left( \frac{\partial \phi}{\partial t} \right)^2 d\mathbf{r} \le 0$$
Hence, $F(t)$ must monotonically decrease along the deterministic trajectory until a stationary state is attained.

## 4. Stationary States and Analytical Solutions

1. **Uniform Fixed Points**:
   - $\phi^* = 0$: Unstable stationary state ($\partial^2 V / \partial \phi^2 = -1 < 0$). Small perturbations grow exponentially.
   - $\phi^* = +1$ and $\phi^* = -1$: Stable ordered equilibrium states ($\partial^2 V / \partial \phi^2 = +2 > 0$).
2. **Stationary Domain Wall (Kink / Antikink)**:
   In 1D, setting $\partial_t \phi = 0$:
   $$\frac{d^2 \phi}{dx^2} + \phi - \phi^3 = 0$$
   With boundary conditions $\phi(-\infty) = \mp 1$ and $\phi(+\infty) = \pm 1$, the exact solution is:
   $$\phi(x) = \pm \tanh\left( \frac{x - x_0}{\sqrt{2}} \right)$$
   The characteristic interface width is:
   $$\xi_{\text{kink}} = \sqrt{2} \approx 1.4142$$
   *Crucial distinction*: Note that a single $\tanh$ is non-periodic on a finite torus $[0, L)$; therefore, evaluation of its spatial truncation error is strictly an **interior spatial truncation test**. True periodic domain walls require paired defects (kink–antikink pairs).
   Furthermore, in Verma et al. (2023) Section VI, the attractive interaction force between well-separated kinks decays as $dR/dt \sim -e^{-R/\xi_{\text{int}}}$, where the asymptotic interaction decay length is $\xi_{\text{int}} = 1/(2\sqrt{2}) \approx 0.3535$. This interaction decay length is distinct from the static profile interface width $\xi_{\text{kink}} = \sqrt{2}$.

## 5. Numerical Discretization and Stability

### Discretization
- **Spatial Derivative (Laplacian)**:
  - 1D: Standard central difference:
    $$\nabla^2 \phi_i \approx \frac{\phi_{i+1} - 2\phi_i + \phi_{i-1}}{(\Delta x)^2}$$
  - 2D: Standard 5-point discrete Laplacian stencil:
    $$\nabla^2 \phi_{i,j} \approx \frac{\phi_{i+1,j} + \phi_{i-1,j} + \phi_{i,j+1} + \phi_{i,j-1} - 4\phi_{i,j}}{(\Delta x)^2}$$
- **Time Stepping**: Explicit Forward Euler:
  $$\phi^{n+1} = \phi^n + \Delta t \left[ \phi^n - (\phi^n)^3 + \nabla_h^2 \phi^n \right]$$
- **Boundary Conditions**: Periodic in all spatial coordinates.

### Numerical Stability Bounds (Yadav et al. 2024, Eq. 35)
Linear stability analysis around the stable fixed point $\phi = \pm 1$ requires avoidance of spurious subharmonic bifurcations:
$$\Delta t \le \frac{(\Delta x)^2}{(\Delta x)^2 + 2D}$$
where $D \in \{1, 2\}$ is the spatial dimension.

#### Parameters from Yadav et al. (2024), Table I:
1. **1D TDGL Simulation**:
   - $L = 100$
   - $N = 1024$
   - $\Delta x = 100/1024 = 0.09765625$
   - $\Delta t_{\text{ref}} = 10^{-3} = 0.001$
   - Stability Limit:
     $$\Delta t_{\max} = \frac{(0.09765625)^2}{(0.09765625)^2 + 2(1)} = \frac{0.0095367}{2.0095367} \approx 4.7457 \times 10^{-3}$$
   - Safety ratio: $\Delta t_{\text{ref}} / \Delta t_{\max} \approx 0.2107 < 1.0$ (Linearly stable around $\phi^* = \pm 1$; note that this is a linear stability condition derived from non-divergence of small perturbations around equilibrium, not an unconditional nonlinear stability guarantee).

2. **2D TDGL Simulation**:
   - $L = 128$
   - $N_x = N_y = 128$
   - $\Delta x = \Delta y = 1.0$
   - $\Delta t_{\text{ref}} = 10^{-2} = 0.01$
   - Stability Limit:
     $$\Delta t_{\max} = \frac{1.0^2}{1.0^2 + 2(2)} = \frac{1}{5} = 0.20$$
   - Safety ratio: $\Delta t_{\text{ref}} / \Delta t_{\max} = 0.05 \ll 1.0$ (Linearly stable with 95% safety margin).

## 6. Initial Conditions

1. **Critical Quench (Zero-mean Random)**:
   $$\phi(\mathbf{r}, 0) = \phi_0 + \delta\phi(\mathbf{r}, 0), \quad \phi_0 = 0, \quad \delta\phi \in [-0.05, 0.05]$$
   Mimics high-temperature paramagnetic phase suddenly quenched to $T < T_c$.
2. **Single Kink**:
   $$\phi(x) = \tanh\left(\frac{x - L/2}{\sqrt{2}}\right)$$
   For numerical convergence testing.
3. **Kink-Antikink Pair**:
   Domain walls separated by distance $R(0)$, demonstrating exponential mutual attraction $dR/dt \sim -e^{-R/\xi}$ and subsequent annihilation into a uniform state.
4. **Biased Off-Critical State**:
   $\phi_0 = 0.2$, $\delta\phi \in [-0.05, 0.05]$. Evolves rapidly on timescale $O(1)$ directly towards uniform magnetization $\phi = +1$.

## 7. Spectral Formulations and Scaling Laws

### Modal Energy
$$E(k, t) = \frac{1}{2} |\hat{\phi}(k, t)|^2$$
where $\hat{\phi}(k, t) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_x \phi(x, t) e^{-i k x}$ (or continuous Fourier equivalent).

### Spectral Evolution & Nonlinear Transfer
$$\frac{\partial E(k, t)}{\partial t} = T(k, t) + 2 E(k, t) - 2 k^2 E(k, t)$$
where the cubic nonlinearity facilitates energy transfer:
$$T(k, t) = -\sum_{k_1+k_2+k_3=k} \text{Re}\left[ \hat{\phi}(k_1) \hat{\phi}(k_2) \hat{\phi}(k_3) \hat{\phi}^*(k) \right]$$

### Asymptotic Coarsening Relations
- In the late-time domain coarsening regime for $\phi_0 = 0$:
  $$T(k, t) \simeq -2 E(k, t) \quad \text{for small } k$$
- Porod's law scaling for sharp interfaces:
  $$E(k, t) \propto k^{-(d+1)}$$
  - In 1D: $E(k) \propto k^{-2}$
  - In 2D: $E(k) \propto k^{-3}$ (or radially averaged shell spectrum $E_{\text{shell}}(k) \propto k \cdot k^{-(d+1)} = k^{-2}$)
